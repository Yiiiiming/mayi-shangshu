# London landmark model references

Checked 2026-10-03. These are original, procedural architectural studies for a driving game, not survey-grade replicas. Source photographs were inspected as reference only; no third-party photograph, production poster, character artwork, or logo is bundled with the game. Theatre signs use newly drawn typography. Heights explicitly identified below follow official published dimensions; other massing and decorative details are approximate visual interpretations. +Z is the principal facade, X is its width, Y is up; units are metres. The model geometry renders locally without a paid mapping account or a streamed 3D service.

## Rendering notes

- `createLandmark(id)` returns a fresh group. `LANDMARK_IDS` lists the supported IDs.
- Materials, primitive geometries and canvas label textures are shared; consumers can merge static meshes by material after placement.
- Principal columns and rounded structures use smooth vertex normals and 32 or more radial samples. Smaller capital ornament and sub-pixel tubes use fewer samples to keep total geometry manageable.
- Opaque reflective glass keeps sorting stable when building meshes are merged. Window recesses use dark glazing, projected mullions and physical stone frames.
- No network access is needed to render a model. In environments without a Canvas 2D context, labels keep their sign material; Big Ben also has geometric fallback clock hands.

## Facade refinement, 2026-10-03

- Buckingham Palace now has a geometric Union Flag, both faces of its cloth colored, rather than a plain blue rectangle. This depicts the palace with the monarch absent; it is not a claim about today's royal residence. [Royal Family: Royal Standard](https://www.royal.uk/encyclopedia/royal-standard?page=10). The East Front follows the verified 23-bay 3:7:3:7:3 rhythm, distinguishes pilasters from pavilion columns, adds first-floor window pediments and a continuous balustraded balcony, and continues windows onto its side elevations. [Historic England: Buckingham Palace](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1239087).
- Admiralty Arch carries the actual two-line Latin inscription, verified against [The Royal Parks](https://www.royalparks.org.uk/visit/parks/st-jamess-park/monuments). The previously invented English name board has been removed. The flanking wings have the different storey arrangements described in the Historic England listing, hipped mansard roofs with dormers, and carved-joint studies around the carriage arches.
- Sondheim's curved corner is now removed from the building mass rather than added as a cylinder projecting beyond its box. Window bands, a curved banner, a roof rail, projecting venue lettering and a low entrance canopy follow that corner. The official operator photograph listed above was checked directly. The broad blue Les Misérables campaign surfaces have original typography; the model does not copy the production's portrait artwork.
- His Majesty's five central window bays now sit behind six Corinthian columns, with the outer giant pilasters and paired half-dormers separated from the central dome. The Charles II Street return also has its own three-window elevation. These features follow the Historic England description.
- Prince Edward's doorway fanlights now have radial metal ribs, the canopy has separate structural ribs and the roof cornice has dentils. Side windows continue the brick frontage. The operator's exterior photograph listed above was inspected directly.
- Chinatown's gate has inscriptions on both sides and independent collision descriptions for all four column bases. Its approximately 15 m overall height is consistent with the builder's report: [FM Conway, Construct, spring 2016, page 13](https://www.fmconway.co.uk/files/library/files/Construct%20Magazine/Construct%20Magazine%20Spring%202016/Construct%20Magazine%20-%20Spring%202016.pdf). Smaller roof details and exact pillar dimensions remain an original visual interpretation.

## Placement and collision metadata

- `bodyBounds` and `facadeZ` describe theatre walls without canopy/ornament projections, in the returned group's recentered coordinates. The renderer can use them to align the actual entrance face with the mapped street frontage.
- `groundSolids` are metre-space wall/pillar boxes with `{ x, z, width, depth, height, rotation? }`. `rotation` is a local Three.js rotation about Y, in radians. Admiralty Arch includes its two rotated wings as well as all six central solid wall sections. Chinatown includes its four bases. The six theatres expose their body collision geometry.
- Sondheim additionally provides the exact sampled `footprint` ring used by its visible rounded mass. Its narrow `groundSolids` follow the same ring, so the renderer need not collide with an invisible rectangular corner.
- `passages.height` is the minimum unobstructed rectangular height. For Admiralty Arch, `maxHeight` separately records the higher centre of the semicircular arch. Chinatown's three openings have a 6.1 m lintel height; its centre opening is 8 m wide in the model. A narrow opening must remain narrow in physics rather than hiding or disabling its columns.
- Label texture caching includes aspect ratio; a venue's wide sign and tall poster can no longer stretch the same canvas into incompatible dimensions.
- Curved mouldings retain 16–96 path segments, and stone balusters use shared turned profiles. The refinements reduce the complete 14-model set from roughly 1.57 million to 1.06 million triangles despite the additional facade detail. All position/normal values were checked for finiteness; raycasts through the five Admiralty passages and three Chinatown openings remain clear at driving height.

## Close entrance pass, 2026-10-03

This pass changes three of the six West End models. It does not assert that their door hardware, pane sizes or paint colours are measured replicas.

- **His Majesty's:** The former solid black canopy is divided into glazed panels, an iron perimeter, transverse ribs and curved supporting brackets. The glass-and-iron canopy is explicitly described in [Historic England's listing](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1357090). Panel spacing and simplified brackets are original interpretations. Below it, the original nine entrance bays now read as paired doors, with transoms, low panels, brass kick plates and pulls.
- **Prince Edward:** Seven existing arched entrance bays retain their dimensions, but now have paired door leaves, restrained green-black metal frames, brass door furniture and an extra concentric fanlight rib. The fanlight geometry and contrasting door metal follow the [official operator's exterior photograph](https://www.delfontmackintosh.co.uk/imgs/media/d6e178c6282b5c6b93edd7ee88f28afb/prince-edward-theatre-beetlejuice-front-of-house-2026.webp), inspected in the first facade pass. No current production artwork is copied.
- **Lyceum:** The three arched entrances gain door-leaf detail. Small dentils and bracket-like modillions under the portico cornice now give its lower edge depth. The three arches and both cornice features are explicitly identified by [Historic England](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1066119). Fine spacing and paint colours remain artistic estimates.

Every model's serialized `userData` was compared with the prior pass: dimensions, centering, `bodyBounds`, `facadeZ`, `footprint`, `groundSolids` and `passages` are unchanged. All position and normal components remain finite. The three edited models add 8,704 triangles altogether (His Majesty's +4.30%, Prince Edward +7.15%, Lyceum +2.49%); the complete landmark set rises from 1,060,077 to 1,068,781 triangles, a 0.82% increase. This pass adds four shared material variants and no downloaded assets, texture requests or paid service.
